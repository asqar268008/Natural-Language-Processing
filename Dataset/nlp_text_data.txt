   NPL usually stands for Non-Performing Loans in finance, 
which are bank loans that are in default or close to default 
because the borrower has not made scheduled interest or 
principal payments for a specified period, typically 90 days. 
When economic downturns occur or borrowers face financial distress, 
NPL ratios rise, forcing banks to write off the bad debt, clear 
it from their balance sheets, or sell it to distressed asset funds. 
High levels of these delinquent loans can severely damage a financial 
institution's profitability, tie up capital that could otherwise be 
used for new lending, and pose broader systemic risks to the entire 
banking sector.
